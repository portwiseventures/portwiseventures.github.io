Pre-shipment inspection: how buyers check quality before goods leave

An inspection before shipment helps confirm that goods match the agreed specification, quantity and packing. Clear written specifications make it work.

Home›Trade Intelligence Centre›Import–Export Regulations & Practice›Pre-shipment inspection: how buyers check quality before goods leave

Updated 24 November 2026 · 1 min read · #inspection · #quality · #AQL

Key takeaways

  • Write the specification and inspection standard down before production.
  • AQL sampling checks a sample, not every unit.
  • Agree the consequences of a failed inspection in advance.

On this page

  1. When inspections happen
  2. Sampling and AQL
  3. What to specify in writing
  4. After the inspection

When inspections happen

  • Before production: checking materials and the supplier's readiness.
  • During production: spotting problems early.
  • Pre-shipment: a final check of finished, packed goods, usually on a random sample.
  • Loading check: confirming the right goods and quantities go into the container.

Sampling and AQL

Most final inspections check a sample rather than every unit. An Acceptable Quality Limit (AQL) sets how many defects in the sample are acceptable. Sampling plans are described in ISO 2859-1. Defects are usually classed as critical, major or minor, each with its own limit.

What to specify in writing

  • Product specification with tolerances, dimensions, materials and performance.
  • Approved sample or reference photographs, kept by both sides.
  • Packaging, marking, labelling and carton or pallet configuration.
  • Test methods and any laboratory tests required, especially for food products and the destination's rules.
  • Defect classification and the AQL levels you accept.

After the inspection

Ask for a written report with photographs and the measured results. Agree in advance what happens if the goods fail: rework and re-inspection, a price adjustment or rejection. Share the inspection standard with the supplier before production starts.
