How to research an export market with free trade data

A short, repeatable method to see who buys a product, how much, from whom and under what conditions, using public trade statistics and tariff databases.

Home›Trade Intelligence Centre›Export Market Insights›How to research an export market with free trade data

Updated 24 November 2026 · 1 min read · #market research · #trade data · #statistics

Key takeaways

  • Start from the right HS code.
  • Combine import volume, trends, competitors and entry conditions.
  • Treat statistics as a starting point and validate with buyers.

On this page

  1. Step 1: define the product by HS code
  2. Step 2: see who imports and from whom
  3. Step 3: compare unit values
  4. Step 4: check conditions of entry
  5. Know the limits of the data

Step 1: define the product by HS code

Trade statistics are organised by HS code. Pick the code, or a few neighbouring codes, that describe your product. Results change a lot depending on how broadly or narrowly you choose.

Step 2: see who imports and from whom

Use a trade statistics database such as ITC Trade Map or UN Comtrade. Look at the value and quantity imported by each country, how those figures have moved over several years, and which countries currently supply the market and in what shares.

Step 3: compare unit values

Dividing value by quantity gives a rough unit value. Comparing unit values between suppliers can hint at whether a market buys on price or quality, but treat it as a clue, not a fact, because product mix varies inside a code.

Step 4: check conditions of entry

Look up the destination's duty rate for your code, any preference you may qualify for and non-tariff requirements such as certificates, labelling or inspections. A market that looks large may be hard to enter for your product.

Know the limits of the data

  • Data lags: the latest year may be incomplete or published months later.
  • Countries report differently. Comparing an exporter's figures with the importer's “mirror” figures can reveal gaps.
  • Codes group different qualities together. Validate with real buyers before you commit.
