International payment terms: advance, letter of credit, collection and open account

How you get paid shapes the risk for both sides. The four common methods sit on a spectrum from most secure for the seller to most secure for the buyer.

Home›Trade Intelligence Centre›Import–Export Regulations & Practice›International payment terms: advance, letter of credit, collection and open account

Updated 24 November 2026 · 1 min read · #payment · #letter of credit · #risk

Key takeaways

  • Payment terms trade risk between buyer and seller.
  • Letters of credit pay only against exactly compliant documents.
  • Keep payment terms, Incoterms and documents consistent.

On this page

  1. The four common methods
  2. Choosing between them
  3. Practical tips

The four common methods

  • Payment in advance: the buyer pays before shipment. Lowest risk for the seller, highest for the buyer.
  • Letter of credit (L/C): a bank undertakes to pay the seller if the documents presented comply exactly with the credit's terms. Documentary credits are commonly governed by ICC's UCP 600.
  • Documentary collection: banks pass documents to the buyer against payment or acceptance, but do not guarantee payment. Commonly governed by ICC's URC 522.
  • Open account: the seller ships first and the buyer pays later. Highest risk for the seller.

Choosing between them

Choose according to trust, the size and value of the order, the country risk and the cost. New relationships often use advance payment for a share of the order, or a letter of credit, then move to less secure terms as trust builds.

Practical tips

  • Make payment terms, Incoterms and required documents consistent in the contract.
  • Under a letter of credit, check the credit's wording against what you can actually supply before accepting it. Small mismatches can delay payment.
  • Ask who pays bank charges, including the other side's bank.
  • Agree the currency and what happens if exchange rates move.
