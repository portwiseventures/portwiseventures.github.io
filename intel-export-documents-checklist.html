Export documents checklist: what a typical shipment needs

A shipment usually travels with a core set of documents, plus extra certificates for food, plants and animal products. Mismatched details are a common cause of delay.

Home›Trade Intelligence Centre›Import–Export Regulations & Practice›Export documents checklist: what a typical shipment needs

Updated 24 November 2026 · 1 min read · #documents · #customs · #export

Key takeaways

  • Consistency across documents matters as much as having them.
  • Food, plant and animal products usually need extra certificates.
  • Confirm destination requirements before production, not after.

On this page

  1. The core documents
  2. Documents that depend on the product or destination
  3. Avoiding delays

The core documents

  • Commercial invoice: seller, buyer, goods, quantities, price, currency, Incoterms and HS code.
  • Packing list: packages, weights, dimensions and marks.
  • Transport document: bill of lading (sea) or air waybill (air).
  • Export and import customs declarations: prepared by the exporter, importer or their brokers.
  • Insurance certificate: if the sales terms make the seller responsible for insurance.

Documents that depend on the product or destination

  • Certificate of origin: when the destination asks for it or a trade preference is claimed.
  • Phytosanitary certificate: commonly required for plants and plant products such as fresh produce.
  • Health or veterinary certificates: commonly required for meat, fish and other animal products.
  • Certificates of analysis, quality or inspection: when the contract, buyer or authority requires them.
  • Letter of credit documents: exactly as the credit specifies.

Avoiding delays

  • Make names, addresses, quantities, weights, descriptions and codes identical across all documents.
  • Ask the importer early which certificates the destination requires for your product.
  • Send draft documents to the buyer or bank before shipping, especially under a letter of credit.
  • Keep copies of everything. Authorities and banks may ask for them later.
