Incoterms 2020 explained for importers and exporters

Incoterms are eleven three-letter rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce. They set out who arranges transport, insurance and customs clearance, and where risk passes from seller to buyer.

Key takeaways

  • Incoterms define delivery, costs and risk, not price, payment or ownership.

  • For container cargo, the any-mode rules (FCA, CPT, CIP) often fit better than FOB, CFR or CIF.

What Incoterms do, and what they do not

Incoterms® rules are published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) and are used in contracts for the sale of goods worldwide. They define who is responsible for transport, insurance, export and import clearance, and at which point the risk of loss or damage moves from the seller to the buyer.

They do not set the price, say when payment is due, transfer ownership of the goods or decide how disputes are resolved. Those points belong in your sales contract.

The eleven rules

Incoterms 2020 groups the rules by transport mode. Seven can be used for any mode of transport, and four are for sea and inland waterway transport only.

  • EXW Ex Works: the buyer collects the goods at the seller's premises.
  • FCA Free Carrier: the seller hands the goods to the buyer's carrier at a named place.
  • CPT Carriage Paid To and CIP Carriage and Insurance Paid To: the seller pays carriage to a named destination (and, for CIP, insurance).
  • DAP Delivered at Place, DPU Delivered at Place Unloaded and DDP Delivered Duty Paid: the seller delivers to the destination, with DDP also covering import duties and clearance.
  • FAS Free Alongside Ship, FOB Free on Board, CFR Cost and Freight and CIF Cost, Insurance and Freight: sea and inland waterway only.

Choosing a rule

The sea-only rules (FOB, CFR, CIF) are written for goods loaded on board a vessel. For containers handed over at a terminal or depot, the any-mode rules (FCA, CPT, CIP) usually describe the real handover point better.

Think about who is better placed to arrange and pay for each step. A buyer with a trusted freight forwarder may prefer FCA or FOB. A buyer who wants one price to a named place may prefer DAP.

Practical tips

  • Write the edition and the place, for example “FCA Chattogram, Incoterms 2020”. A rule without a named place is ambiguous.
  • Check who clears the goods for export and for import. This differs between rules.
  • Insurance cover differs: under Incoterms 2020, CIP calls for a higher minimum level of cover than CIF.
  • Use the same rule on the quotation, contract, invoice and shipping documents.
